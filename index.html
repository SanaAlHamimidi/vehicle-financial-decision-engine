Vehicle Financial Decision Engine

A regional depreciation and resale value analysis of the California used car market, combining 50,000+ vehicle listings, 3.6 million DMV registration records, and machine learning to quantify how geography, fuel type, and vehicle characteristics influence long-term financial value.

Three key research questions are investigated:

  1. Which vehicle features affect resale price the most?
  2. How does geography in California affect resale value?
  3. Does selling the same vehicle acorss California region yield different resale prices?
Nyi Wai Yan Tun Sana Al Hamimidi San José State University XGBoost · K-Means · Demand Engineering

3.6M registration records across 6 years

Key Findings
What the data revealed about California's used car market
📊

What Drives Resale Price

01
Fuel type is the #1 predictor (27% importance)

Diesel vehicles average $32,001 — the highest of any fuel type — driven by truck dominance. Fuel type alone is not a clean signal without controlling for vehicle type.

02
Year and odometer are strong secondary drivers

Year has a correlation of 0.59 with price. Odometer has a correlation of -0.58. Together they explain most of the depreciation signal.

03
Condition has a sharp impact at the low end

New condition averages $24,561 while fair condition averages only $3,796 — a 85% drop. Condition matters most when a car is in poor shape.

🗺️

Geography Matters More Than You Think

04
Same car, $25,000 price difference by region

A Chevrolet truck in Imperial County averages $45,645 vs $17,095 in Los Angeles — a $28,000 difference for the same brand purely due to regional demand.

05
Agricultural regions have the lowest depreciation

Imperial County (-0.10), Bakersfield (0.08), and Visalia-Tulare (0.13) show the lowest depreciation rates — driven by high demand for work trucks.

06
Urban markets depreciate faster due to supply

Los Angeles (0.38) and Inland Empire (0.41) show higher depreciation despite higher incomes — more listings means more competition and lower prices.

🚗

Depreciation by Brand and Type

07
Trucks appreciate — not depreciate

RAM (0.005), GMC (0.13), and Ford F-150 (0.16) show near-zero or negligible depreciation. Heavy duty trucks have appreciated above their original MSRP post-2020.

08
Tesla holds value best among passenger cars

Tesla shows a depreciation rate of only 0.08 — the lowest of any passenger vehicle brand — reflecting California's strong EV demand.

09
Discontinued brands depreciate fastest

Mercury (0.84), Pontiac (0.79), and Saturn (0.78) show the highest depreciation rates — no manufacturer support means rapidly falling demand.

⚡

The EV Shift Is Already Happening

10
Battery Electric registrations grew 6x from 2019-2024

From 226,614 registered EVs in 2019 to 1,459,536 in 2024. California's EV transition is accelerating faster than most buyers realize.

11
Gasoline registrations declined by 2 million

From 26.9M in 2019 to 24.7M in 2024. Buying a gasoline car today means buying into a shrinking market — which affects long-term resale value.

12
Fuel type alone does not tell the full story

Diesel tops average resale prices but this is driven by truck dominance, not diesel passenger cars. Vehicle type must be considered alongside fuel type.

The Model
XGBoost trained on 37,991 California vehicle listings

Model Performance

0.779 R² Score
$5,891 Avg Error (RMSE)
37,991 Training rows

The model explains 77.9% of used car price variation in California — consistent with industry standard performance for used car price prediction (0.75–0.85 range).

XGBoost Regressor 100 estimators Max depth 6 Learning rate 0.1

Features Used

Core vehicle features

Year, odometer, manufacturer, condition, fuel type, vehicle type, region

Engineered demand score

Calculated from 6 years of DMV registration data by region, make and fuel type. Captures how in-demand a specific vehicle is in a specific California area.

What the model does NOT use

MSRP and depreciation rate were excluded to prevent data leakage — the model learns from genuine market signals only.

Project Limitations & Future Work

Known Limitations
  • Craigslist listings may not represent dealer or auction markets
  • Brand-level MSRP averaging inflates depreciation for some economy brands
  • MSRP dataset covers 13 brands — remaining brands use proxy values
  • Model explains 77.9% of price variation — 22% remains unexplained
Future Improvements
  • Model-level MSRP matching for more accurate depreciation estimates
  • Incorporate accident history and number of owners
  • Expand to other California data sources beyond Craigslist
  • Add temporal depreciation curves to show value over time
