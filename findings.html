Major Findings
Key results addressing our three research questions

Q1 — Which vehicle features affect resale price the most?

XGBoost feature importance and correlation analysis reveal the relative contribution of each feature to resale price prediction.

0.779 R² Score — model explains 77.9% of price variation
$5,891 RMSE — average prediction error
27% Fuel type importance — the top predictor
01
Fuel type is the strongest predictor at 27% importance

Diesel vehicles average $32,001 — the highest of any fuel type — driven by truck dominance in that category. This reveals that fuel type alone is not a clean signal without controlling for vehicle type.

02
Year (0.59 correlation) and odometer (-0.58) are strong secondary drivers

Confirming that vehicle age and mileage are the primary depreciation drivers. Together with fuel type they account for over 70% of the model's predictive power.

03
Condition has a sharp impact at the low end

New condition averages $24,561 while fair condition averages only $3,796 — an 85% drop. The financial penalty for poor condition is severe and non-linear.

Q2 — How does geography in California affect resale value?

Regional depreciation rates reveal significant geographic variation in how vehicles hold value across California.

04
Siskiyou County has the highest depreciation rate at 0.61

Remote rural areas with limited demand show the fastest value loss. Palm Springs (0.44) and Inland Empire (0.41) follow, driven by high supply and market competition in those areas.

05
Agricultural regions show the lowest depreciation — Imperial County actually appreciates at -0.10

Bakersfield (0.08) and Visalia-Tulare (0.13) also show near-zero depreciation. High demand for work trucks in agricultural communities drives prices above average MSRP.

06
SF Bay Area sits in the middle at 0.31 despite high EV adoption

The high volume of listings in the Bay Area creates competitive pricing pressure that offsets the area's high income levels, resulting in moderate rather than low depreciation rates.

Q3 — Does selling the same vehicle across regions yield different prices?

Analysis of truck resale prices by region reveals substantial geographic price variation for identical vehicle brands.

07
Same brand truck, $28,000 price difference by region

A Chevrolet truck in Imperial County averages $45,645 while the same brand in Los Angeles averages $17,095 — a difference of $28,550 for the same manufacturer. Geography is a significant financial variable for sellers.

08
Central Valley regions consistently outperform urban areas for truck resale

Hanford-Corcoran ($43,000), Bakersfield ($29,662), and Visalia-Tulare ($28,248) rank highest for truck resale prices. Agricultural demand for work vehicles sustains premium pricing in these markets.

09
Urban markets have higher supply and lower truck resale prices

Los Angeles ($17,095), Modesto ($18,760), and Inland Empire ($20,005) rank lowest for truck resale. More listings means more competition which drives prices down regardless of brand.

Brand Depreciation Patterns

Discontinued brands depreciate fastest

Mercury (0.84), Pontiac (0.79), Saturn (0.78) — no manufacturer support accelerates value loss.

Tesla holds value best among passenger cars

Depreciation rate of 0.08 — reflecting strong California EV demand and brand loyalty.

Trucks appreciate — RAM at 0.005, F-150 at 0.16

Post-2020 supply chain disruptions drove used truck prices above original MSRP in many cases.

EV Market Shift (2019–2024)

Battery Electric registrations grew 6x

From 226,614 in 2019 to 1,459,536 in 2024 — California's EV transition is accelerating.

Gasoline registrations declined by 2.25 million

From 26.9M in 2019 to 24.7M in 2024. Buying gasoline today means entering a shrinking market.
